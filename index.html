The Math of Risk-to-Reward (1:3 R:R)

In a 1:3 Risk-to-Reward ratio, your potential profit is 3x your potential loss per trade.

  • Sample Size: 10 Trades
  • Risk per trade: $100
  • Reward per trade: $300
  • Win Rate: 30% (3 Wins, 7 Losses)

Losses: 7 trades × -$100 = -$700

Wins: 3 trades × +$300 = +$900

Net Profit: +$900 - $700 = +$200

You only need to be right 3 out of 10 times to remain net profitable.

Core Concepts

What is Sample Size?

A statistical sample size (e.g., 20 to 100 executed trades) is required to test a system. A outcome from 1 or 2 trades is random noise; consistency can only be measured over a significant set of trades without altering rules mid-way.

What is an Edge?

An edge is a statistical advantage that produces a positive expected value (EV) over a large sample size. It comes from a combination of win rate, risk-to-reward ratio, and disciplined execution.

How Execution Affects a System

A system with a high theoretical edge will fail if poor execution introduces slippage, emotional entries, early exits, or missed setups. Perfect execution turns a statistical edge into real-world profit.

"A profitable trading system is easy to find, but hard to follow. Most traders fail not because the strategy is wrong, but because they lack patience to stick with it. Quick profits lead to overtrading and revenge trades. Consistent profits come from discipline, patience, and trusting the process."
