"Don't judge a trade by its outcome; judge it by your execution. Following your rules on a loss is a good trade; breaking your rules for a win is just a lucky gamble."

"Outcomes don't define trade quality—execution does. A disciplined loss is a good trade; an undisciplined win is a bad habit."

Knowledge Base: Risk to Reward Ratio Formula

When analyzing a sample size of 50 trades, your win rate determines how aggressive your risk-to-reward (R:R) ratio needs to be to remain profitable. Assuming you risk a fixed amount of $100 per trade (where a loss always equals -$100), here is how different risk-to-reward ratios perform over 50 trades with 30 wins (60% win rate) versus 25 wins (50% win rate):

Risk-to-Reward Ratio 30 Wins / 20 Losses (60% Win Rate) 25 Wins / 25 Losses (50% Win Rate)
1:1 RR (Win: $100 / Loss: $100) +$1,000 Profit $0 (Break-Even)
1:2 RR (Win: $200 / Loss: $100) +$4,000 Profit +$2,500 Profit
1:3 RR (Win: $300 / Loss: $100) +$7,000 Profit +$5,000 Profit

Scenario A: 30 Wins and 20 Losses (60% Win Rate)

At 1:1 R:R: You make $100 on 30 wins ($3,000) and lose $100 on 20 losses (-$2,000).

Net Profit = $3,000 - $2,000 = +$1,000

At 1:2 R:R: You make $200 on 30 wins ($6,000) and lose $100 on 20 losses (-$2,000).

Net Profit = $6,000 - $2,000 = +$4,000

Scenario B: 25 Wins and 25 Losses (50% Win Rate)

At 1:1 R:R: You make $100 on 25 wins ($2,500) and lose $100 on 25 losses (-$2,500).

Net Profit = $2,500 - $2,500 = $0 (Break-even)

At 1:2 R:R: You make $200 on 25 wins ($5,000) and lose $100 on 25 losses (-$2,500).

Net Profit = $5,000 - $2,500 = +$2,500

The Takeaway: With a 50% win rate, a 1:1 R:R leaves you with exactly zero growth after fees and slippage. Bumping your setup to a 1:2 or 1:3 R:R ensures that even a coin-flip win rate yields strong profitability over a 50-trade sample size.
